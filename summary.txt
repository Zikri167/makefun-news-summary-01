World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026
1. Russian, Ukrainian and US officials began a second day of peace talks in Abu Dhabi, the first trilateral meeting since February. Tuesday's session covered prisoner exchanges and was called constructive, with no breakthrough. (Reuters)
2. World leaders opened COP31 in Antalya, where nearly 200 countries negotiate until 20 November. Turkey wants electricity at 35% of global energy by 2035; the US is absent again. (Reuters)
3. Britain's UKMTO reported more tanker incidents in the Strait of Hormuz; Iran's Revolutionary Guards said tankers hit mines on "unauthorised routes". Oil rose. (Reuters)
4. British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer near Gloucester on suspicion of spying for Russia, and the US charged three men over an alleged Iran-backed plot on RAF Fairford. (CNN)
5. The US House voted 211-204 to shelve impeachment of Defence Secretary Hegseth. Republicans picked Jim Jordan as House leader and re-elected John Thune in the Senate. (PBS)
6. US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, core inflation 0.3% for a third month, above forecasts. A December Fed rise looks near-certain, with a half-point move possible. (BLS; Reuters)
7. Anthropic began its Nasdaq IPO roadshow, offering 825 million shares at $70-$78 to raise up to $64bn and value the Claude maker near $1.4tn fully diluted. Trading as ANT is expected on 19 November. (Reuters; CNBC)
8. President Bukele said Salvadoran troops and Haitian police destroyed the 400 Mawozo gang's headquarters near Port-au-Prince; sources put the toll near 20, unconfirmed. (Miami Herald)
9. Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,000 cases, 5,387 deaths, the second-largest on record, government data showed. Kenya discharged its last Ebola patient, starting a 42-day countdown to being declared disease-free. (Reuters)
10. A bus hit 32 vehicles at a traffic light in Seongnam, South Korea, injuring 57, nine seriously. The driver said the brakes had failed. (Yonhap)
